# ANDOR/OSP: features, benefits and how it compares

Source material for the website and landing page. It is written in the product's own voice.
Every feature listed is in the current development build, unless a line says otherwise.
Section 7 lists what to say carefully and what to check before publishing.

---

## 1. The one-line story

> **Drop in a sound. Play an instrument.**

ANDOR/OSP (One Shot Performer) turns a single recording into a playable instrument across
the whole keyboard. The recording can be a sung vowel, a bowed note, an old synth, an organ,
a pluck or a phone memo. There is no mapping, no zones and no preparation. The goal is not
lab realism. It is an instrument you want to play, that still sounds like *your* sound.

**Short versions**

- *One sample. A whole instrument.*
- *Your sound, played: alive, sustained, reimagined.*
- *From a voice memo to an instrument in seconds.*

**Who it is for**

- Producers and songwriters who record their own sounds.
- Film and game composers who want a signature palette.
- Sound designers.
- Anyone with a folder of one-shots they never turned into anything.

The creative territory is acoustic/electronic hybrids, cinematic sampling, and the charm
of old sampling keyboards rebuilt with modern sound.

## 3. Features and benefits

Each entry gives the feature, then why a musician cares.

### Getting sound in

- **Drop one file, play instantly.**
  - WAV, AIFF or FLAC; mono or stereo; 44.1–96 kHz.
  - The root note is detected for you; if a sound has no clear pitch, it asks.
  - *Benefit:* the idea stays alive. No setup between hearing a sound and playing it.
- **Live capture: record or grab what you just played.**
  - Each card has a REC key. RECORD a take up to a minute, or GRAB the LAST 10, 20, 30 or
    60 seconds after the fact.
  - Sources are a microphone or interface input (standalone), or a DAW track sent to the
    plugin's sidechain.
  - The take is trimmed and chopped into its notes, and each note is mapped to its key
    automatically.
  - *Benefit:* sing, hum or play a phrase and play it back as an instrument straight away.
    The happy accident is never lost.
- **MAP NOTES / Note Atlas: one phrase becomes many samples.**
  - A recording with several notes is analysed into its separate notes, each placed on its
    own key.
  - The NOTE MAP lets you edit, split, merge, retune, add velocity layers and FILL the
    keyboard.
  - *Benefit:* a melody you recorded once becomes a multi-sampled instrument with the real
    note-to-note character of the performance.
- **Multi-sample intelligence.**
  - Drop a folder: pitches, velocity layers and round robins are sorted out automatically.
  - Velocity layers are blended so boundaries don't jump.
  - *Benefit:* the power of a deep sampler without its mapping workflow.
- **The Library.**
  - Every sound you load is kept, searchable and auditionable, with collections, tags and
    favourites.
  - Folder scanning that recognises likely one-shots.
  - An Inbox that watches your Voice Memos, Dropbox or iCloud folder.
  - Trim and slice; packs; full backup and restore.
  - *Benefit:* your sounds stop getting lost on drives. The ones you recorded on your phone
    yesterday are already waiting.

### Making it play like an instrument

- **LIFE, the performance engine.**
  - Each note is a slightly different performance: level, brightness, body, attack,
    micro-pitch and stereo, varied together the way a player varies them.
  - Variation is correlated through force, colour and timing, not independent random dice.
  - Calibrated on real repeated takes. 0 % is identical every time; 50 % is a believable
    player; 100 % is freer reinterpretation.
  - Optional fixed round robins (TAKES 2–16).
  - *Benefit:* repeated notes and chords stop sounding like a machine gun.
- **Continuation: sustain forever, never hear the loop.**
  - The recording's own steady part is found and walked through several matched loops that
    never repeat back-to-back, with correlation-aware crossfades.
  - When you let go, the note joins the recording's real ending (release grafting).
  - *Benefit:* a two-second note becomes a pad you can hold for a minute.
- **Dynamics that change the sound.**
  - Velocity moves the transient, brightness, attack bite and body, not only the level.
  - A playable curve (SOFT / LINEAR / HARD).
  - *Benefit:* one sample responds like a real instrument from pianissimo to forte.
- **Two pitch characters.**
  - *Tape*: classic resampling, faster and brighter going up.
  - *Natural*: keeps the recording's speed and movement in other registers.
  - *Benefit:* choose the vintage-sampler charm or a more natural instrument per patch.
- **Expressive control.**
  - MPE (per-note bend ±48 semitones, pressure, slide), aftertouch, mod wheel, CC macros.
  - Mono mode with legato and glide for basses and leads.

### Transforming it

- **REIMAGINED, five modes per layer.** One knob takes each source from the recording
  itself to its mode's far end:
  - **KALEIDOSCOPE**: sympathetic resonance tuned to the sound, a fluid sustain, moving
    doubling, wandering formants, grains at the far end.
  - **TAPE FRAME**: the sound laid on a finite piece of tape. Low notes are slower and darker,
    and every pass is a little more worn.
  - **TOYBOX**: a tiny, clever digital memory whose head turns back and forth through the
    sound.
  - **MOSAIC**: the sound rebuilt from its harmonic fingerprint, at any pitch, travelling
    through the recording's own evolution.
  - **MIRAGE**: early-digital weight. The sample clock follows the note, into a driven
    resonant 4-pole filter.
  - *Benefit:* five distinct instruments hidden in every sample, each still recognisably the
    source.
- **Granular mode with KEY DNA.**
  - Any layer becomes a cloud of grains: POS, SIZE, DENS, TUNE, SPREAD, SCATTER, PAN, three
    windows.
  - KEY DNA gives each key of the keyboard its own grain behaviour: drawn curves per
    parameter, variation, individuality.
  - *Benefit:* evolving textures that change with where you play, not one static cloud.
- **SYNTHESIZE: an analog oscillator that plays your sound's notes.**
  - A warm, band-limited oscillator (saw, square, triangle, sine; octave, detune, tone) blended
    into each source.
  - It follows the keys, or the recording's own tuning and vibrato.
  - *Benefit:* add weight, sustain and synth body under an acoustic recording, from subtle to
    fully synthetic.
- **IMPRINT: the recording's spectral body laid onto the oscillator.**
  - The source's own spectral envelope (resonant body, formants, bow or breath colour) shapes
    the oscillator.
  - MOTION follows how that colour changes through the note.
  - Every note of an Atlas carries its own imprint.
  - *Benefit:* a synth voice that sounds like it was cut from your recording. It keeps the
    recording's identity even when the sample itself is gone, and holds after a pluck has
    died away.
- **Three layers (A, B, C).**
  - Up to three sounds play together as one instrument.
  - Each layer has its own mode, REIMAGINED, EQ, DAMPEN, SYNTH and arpeggiator lane, with MIX
    and LINK between them.
  - *Benefit:* build hybrid instruments (a sung vowel, a bowed tail, a synth body) without
    a separate sampler for each.

### Shaping and space

- **Six macros, each with its own visual popover:** LIFE, DRIVE, CHARACTER, MOVEMENT, ECHO,
  SPACE.
- **DRIVE**: TUBE, TAPE and CRUNCH circuits, level-matched, reacting to how hard they're fed
  like hardware.
- **CHARACTER**: a filter that moves the source's own body resonances.
- **MOVEMENT**: DRIFT, TAPE wow and flutter, BBD CHORUS, PULSE, and the SHAPER (tempo-locked
  rhythmic patterns, or your own drawn pattern).
- **ECHO**: TAPE, BBD and ASCEND. ASCEND puts a pitch shifter in the delay loop, so repeats
  climb an interval.
- **SPACE, four reverb engines:**
  - ROOMS (room, hall, plate, spring);
  - ABYSS (huge, swelling, freezable; DEEP / DARK / INVERSE / ENDLESS);
  - VINTAGE (early-digital hall, plate, chamber, room);
  - BLOOM (diffusing, tempo-synced echoes into reverb).
- **Per-layer EQ and DAMPEN**: a five-band EQ drawn on the waveform, and key-dependent damping
  across the keyboard.
- **Modulation**:
  - sources: two LFOs (custom shapes, poly or global, sync), two envelopes, the mod wheel;
  - 16 routes, drag-and-drop onto any knob;
  - modulation of a modulation's depth.
- **Arpeggiator, per card**: 18 patterns, rate, gate, octaves, swing, host-synced. Each layer
  can arpeggiate on its own, or all linked.

### Workflow and trust

- **Projects reopen exactly.** The sample, corrections and settings are stored; bounces render
  identically every time, because all variation comes from stored seeds.
- **Presets, starting states and portable instruments.** An `.ospinstrument` carries the
  recordings, their analysis and settings, and sounds the same on another computer.
- **Offline and private.** No account, no cloud, no network needed. Your audio never leaves
  your machine.
- **Formats.** AU, VST3 and Standalone on macOS (Apple Silicon and Intel). Windows VST3 builds
  and passes its tests in CI but is not distributed yet (see section 7).
- **Interface.** Scales 80–200 %. Undo/redo for sound changes too, not only knobs.
- **Fails beautifully.** Odd input (noise, no pitch, tiny or huge files) never crashes. It
  plays as well as it can and tells you why.

## 4. Use-case stories (for landing-page sections)

1. **"The voice memo."** You hum a phrase into your phone on a walk. At home it is already in
   OSP's Inbox. MAP NOTES turns it into a playable instrument of your own voice. Add LIFE,
   hold a chord, and the sustain never loops.
2. **"The one perfect note."** One bowed nyckelharpa note becomes a whole-keyboard
   instrument. TAPE FRAME gives it a faded-film version; IMPRINT under it adds a synth body
   that still sounds like the instrument.
3. **"Grab what just happened."** You improvised something great on your guitar. GRAB LAST
   30 s, and it's an instrument you can play back.
4. **"Hybrid patch in a minute."** A: a vocal vowel; B: an old synth note in Granular with
   KEY DNA; C: a pluck through MOSAIC. ARP on B only, ABYSS on the space.
5. **"The sampler that remembers."** Every sound you've ever dropped in is in the Library,
   tagged and searchable, with packs and backups.

## 5. How OSP compares

OSP is not trying to be Kontakt, Falcon or a modular sampler. It does a narrower thing very
well: **from one recording you own to an expressive instrument, with no mapping work.** Keep
that framing on the website. Compare *approaches*, not who has more features.

### 5.1 By category

| What you want | Typical DAW sampler (stock) | Deep sampler / library player | Granular or hybrid synth | **ANDOR/OSP** |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Play one recording across the keyboard | Yes, usually needs root and loop set by hand | Yes, via zone mapping | Often, as an oscillator or source | **Yes: drop and play, root detected** |
| Sustain a short note without an audible loop | Manual loop points or warping | Depends on the library's programming | Granular sustain changes the sound | **Continuation: multi-loop sustain on the recording itself, release grafting** |
| Repeated notes that don't sound identical | Basic randomisation (pitch/volume/filter), or round robins you must record | Round robins the library provides | Random modulation | **LIFE: correlated per-note performance, calibrated on real takes; round robins from one sample** |
| Velocity that changes timbre | Filter or volume mapping | If the library recorded layers | Mod-matrix routing | **Built in: transient, brightness, bite, body from one sample** |
| Turn a melodic phrase into mapped notes | Slicing to pads/keys (by transient) | Manual | Rarely | **MAP NOTES / Note Atlas: pitch-aware notes on their own keys, editable** |
| Capture from input after the fact | Usually record first, then load | No | No | **GRAB LAST 10–60 s straight into mapped notes** |
| Creative transformation that keeps identity | Effects after the sampler | Library-specific | Wide, but often loses the source | **Five REIMAGINED modes, one knob from faithful to far** |
| Synth layer derived from the sample | Separate synth needed | Separate | Some resynthesise (additive/spectral) | **SYNTHESIZE + IMPRINT: an oscillator coloured by the recording's own spectral body, per note** |
| Setup effort | Low to medium | High (or none, with presets only) | Medium to high | **Very low: drop a file** |
| Your own sounds | Yes | Varies (some can't import) | Yes | **Yes, central. Library, Inbox, packs** |

### 5.2 Named products (positioning notes)

Fair notes for comparison pages and FAQs. **Check every competitor claim against current

| Product | What it does well | Where OSP is different |
|---|---|---|
| **Ableton Simpler / Sampler** | Fast slicing and warping inside Live; deep zone editing in Sampler | OSP adds automatic sustain without loops, performance variation (LIFE), REIMAGINED transformations and phrase-to-notes mapping. It works in any DAW. |
| **Logic Quick Sampler / Sampler** | Quick Sampler can trim, detect pitch and loop automatically. Sampler maps zones; Logic also includes Sample Alchemy for resynthesis | OSP's sustain is a continuation of the recording rather than a loop. Every note is performed differently. A sung phrase becomes mapped notes. IMPRINT gives a synth layer with the recording's body. It is not tied to Logic. |
| **Kontakt and library players** (incl. Spitfire LABS-style instruments) | Huge, deeply programmed libraries; scripting | OSP is about *your* sound with zero programming. LABS-style players don't load your own recordings. |
| **Granular synths** (Pigments, Omnisphere, Phase Plant, Serum 2's granular, Ableton Granulator) | Deep sound design, big modulation systems | OSP keeps the source recognisable by default. Granular is one mode among several, with KEY DNA for per-key behaviour. The instrument is playable without patching. |
| **UVI Falcon** | A modular hybrid instrument with many engines | Falcon is a builder's toolkit; OSP is an instrument with opinions. |
| **Synplant 2 (Genopatch)** | Generates synth patches that imitate a dropped sample | OSP keeps the recording itself and lays its spectral body onto an oscillator (IMPRINT), blended with the sample. It also offers sustain, performance and transformation of the sample. |

### 5.3 What OSP is uniquely combining

As far as we know, no single instrument combines all of these. Phrase it as "the
combination" rather than "the only one that…":

1. One-file instant instrument, **plus**
2. Loop-free continuation sustain, **plus**
3. Calibrated per-note performance variation (LIFE), **plus**
4. Phrase → mapped notes, including **grab-after-the-fact live capture**, **plus**
5. Five identity-keeping transformation modes, **plus**
6. An analog oscillator **imprinted with the recording's own spectral body**, per note,
7. All offline, private and deterministic.

## 6. Proof points (for credibility blocks)

- Variation (LIFE) was calibrated on real repeated takes. In blind listening it beat
  identical repeats on every non-vocal test source.
- The continuation sustain was tested with 60-second holds; the loop was mostly inaudible
  (rated 4–5 of 5 on non-vocal sources).
- The pitch strategy was chosen by blind listening between three engines.
- Every build runs thousands of automated tests. These include:
  - bit-identical recall of projects;
  - host robustness at 22.05–192 kHz and block sizes from 1 to 4096 samples;
  - no memory allocation on the audio thread.
- The same seed gives the same output, so your bounces are repeatable.

## 7. Say carefully and check before publishing

**Release status** (as of 8 Nov 2026):
- Development builds only: unsigned, not notarised, no installer.
- Windows VST3 builds and passes tests in CI but is not shipped.
- Host validation in Logic, Ableton and Reaper on real Macs is still to do.

**Factory sounds:** none yet. Example content needs legally owned recordings.

**Results still under listening review:**
- IMPRINT was measured on stand-in references; the musical verdict on real recordings is
  pending.
- Some earlier listening verdicts were mixed:
  - Dynamics on sustained sources;
  - the Reimagined range (it was rebuilt afterwards).

  Say "designed to", not "proven to".

**CPU:**
- IMPRINT adds roughly 120–150 ns per voice per sample on the test machine.
- Avoid "zero CPU" style claims. "Costs nothing when off" is accurate.

**Competitor claims (section 5.2):**
- These come from general product knowledge, not a fresh audit. Check each against the
  current version and its documentation before publishing.
- Logic in particular has strong automatic features (Quick Sampler's optimisation, Sample
  Alchemy).
- Prefer "approach" language to "they can't".

**Unverified "first/only" claims:** don't use them. Use "the combination" (5.3).

**Name:** ANDOR/OSP (One Shot Performer). Sister instruments from the same platform include
ANDOR ANALOG (a synthesizer).
