## DeepDive · Features (one list, used by all six overlays)

### 1 · Your recording  (id `recording`, media panel)

- Kicker: Getting sound in
- Body: Drop in one WAV, AIFF or FLAC file. OSP finds its root note and the sound is playable while the analysis finishes. Add a second and a third sound, and all three play as one instrument.
- Facts: WAV · AIFF · FLAC · 44.1–96 kHz · Up to 3 layers · No mapping
- View 1: **Before** · image `20-deep-dives__dd-recording-empty.webp`
- View 2: **Three layers** · image `20-deep-dives__dd-recording-loaded.webp`

### 2 · LIFE  (id `life`, media popover)

- Kicker: The performance engine
- Body: Each note is a slightly different performance. Level, brightness, body, attack, micro-pitch and stereo move together, the way a player’s do. At 0 % every note is identical, around 50 % it plays like a believable player, at 100 % more freely.
- Facts: Natural · Loose · Fray · Takes 2–16 · Pitch · Tone · Attack
- View 1: **LIFE** · image `20-deep-dives__dd-life.webp`

### 3 · SYNTH + IMPRINT  (id `synth`, media panel)

- Kicker: Synthesis from your sound
- Body: A warm analog oscillator blends under each layer and follows the keys, or the recording’s own tuning and vibrato.
- Facts: Per layer · Keys or recording pitch · Costs nothing when off
- View 1: **Analog** · image `20-deep-dives__dd-synth-analog.webp` · text: ANALOG: saw, square, triangle or sine, with octave, detune and tone, blended under the recording from subtle to fully synthetic.
- View 2: **Imprint** · image `20-deep-dives__dd-synth-imprint.webp` · text: IMPRINT: the recording’s spectral body (its resonances, formants, bow or breath colour) shapes the oscillator, so the synth sounds cut from your sound.
- View 3: **Imprint + Motion** · image `20-deep-dives__dd-synth-motion.webp` · text: MOTION: the imprint follows how that colour changes through the note. Every note of a mapped phrase carries its own imprint.

### 4 · REIMAGINED  (id `reimagined`, media popover)

- Kicker: Five instruments in every sample
- Body: One knob takes each layer from the recording itself toward its mode’s far end, and the sound stays recognisably yours.
- Facts: —
- View 1: **Kaleidoscope** · image `20-deep-dives__dd-reimagined-kaleidoscope.webp` · text: KALEIDOSCOPE: sympathetic resonance tuned to the sound, a fluid sustain, moving doubling and wandering formants, with grains at the far end.
- View 2: **Tape Frame** · image `20-deep-dives__dd-reimagined-tape-frame.webp` · text: TAPE FRAME: the sound laid on a finite piece of tape. Low notes are slower and darker, and every pass is a little more worn.
- View 3: **Toybox** · image `20-deep-dives__dd-reimagined-toybox.webp` · text: TOYBOX: a tiny, clever digital memory whose head turns back and forth through the sound.
- View 4: **Mosaic** · image `20-deep-dives__dd-reimagined-mosaic.webp` · text: MOSAIC: the sound rebuilt from its harmonic fingerprint, at any pitch, travelling through the recording’s own evolution.
- View 5: **Mirage** · image `20-deep-dives__dd-reimagined-mirage.webp` · text: MIRAGE: early-digital weight. The sample clock follows the note into a driven, resonant 4-pole filter.

### 5 · SPACE  (id `space`, media popover)

- Kicker: Four reverb engines
- Body: ROOMS gives you room, hall, plate and spring. ABYSS is huge, swelling and freezable. VINTAGE brings early-digital halls, plates and chambers, and BLOOM diffuses tempo-synced echoes into reverb.
- Facts: Rooms · Abyss · Vintage · Bloom
- View 1: **SPACE** · image `20-deep-dives__dd-space.webp`

### 6 · Keyboard + arpeggiator  (id `keyboard`, media panel)

- Kicker: Play it, or let it play
- Body: Play chords and hold notes longer than the recording lasts. The arpeggiator has 18 patterns with rate, gate, octaves and swing, synced to your host, and each layer can arpeggiate on its own or all together.
- Facts: 18 patterns · Per layer or linked · MPE · aftertouch
- View 1: **Arpeggiator** · image `20-deep-dives__dd-arp.webp`

## Deeper features (native)

- **Note Atlas**: One phrase becomes many samples. Record a melody once. MAP NOTES finds each note, places it on its own key and keeps the real note-to-note character of the performance. Edit, split, merge, retune and fill the keyboard. Image `08-features__feat-notemap.webp`
- **Live Capture**: Grab what you just played. Every card has a REC key. Record a take of up to a minute, or grab the last 10, 20, 30 or 60 seconds after the fact, from a microphone, an interface or a DAW track. It is chopped into notes and mapped for you. Image `08-features__feat-capture.webp`
- **KEY DNA**: Every key its own grain. In Granular mode, KEY DNA gives each key its own grain behaviour: curves you draw across the keyboard, variation and individuality, so a texture changes with where you play. Image `08-features__feat-keydna.webp`

## FAQ

**Does it work with my DAW?**

ANDOR OSP is being built as AU, VST3 and Standalone for macOS. Before release it will be validated in Logic Pro, Ableton Live and Reaper, and we will list every host it has been tested in.

**Can I use my own sounds?**

Yes, that is the point. Drop in any WAV, AIFF or FLAC recording, record from an input, or grab the last seconds you played. A phone memo works as well as a studio recording.

**What kind of recording works best?**

Anything with a pitch: a voice, a bowed or plucked string, an organ, an old synth, a sustained texture. Unusual sounds still play; if a sound has no clear pitch, OSP asks you for the key instead of refusing it.

**How is it different from my DAW’s sampler?**

A sampler maps and loops what you give it. OSP starts from one recording and makes it an instrument for you: it finds the root, sustains past the end of the recording, performs each note a little differently, and can transform it while it stays recognisably your sound.

**Does it need the internet?**

No. Everything works offline, there is no account, and your audio never leaves your computer.

**What does it cost, and when is it out?**

Pricing and the release date will be announced together. Join the waitlist and we will write when it is ready to play.

**Windows?**

The first release is for macOS. Windows has not been announced yet.
