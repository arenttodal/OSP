A closer look at ANDOR OSP.

Hold it for a minute.

Pedal Loop keeps a held note going on the sustain pedal, ping-ponging through the recording's steady part, so a two-second sound becomes a pad.

A cloud of the sound.

Any layer becomes grains: POS, SIZE from 20 to 400 ms, DENS from 4 to 40 a second, SPREAD, SCATTER, PAN. Here card B, while POS and SPREAD move.

Every key its own grain.

KEY DNA draws a curve across the keyboard for each grain parameter, with depth and variation. Low keys can blur while high keys glitter.

A synth with your sound's body.

SYNTH blends a warm oscillator into the source. IMPRINT lays the recording's resonances and formants onto it, and MOTION follows how they change through the note.

Chords, strummed.

STRUM spreads a chord step across time at the speed you set, on any of the three arpeggiators.

Take it anywhere.

An .ospinstrument carries the recordings, their analysis and every setting, and sounds the same on another computer.

Questions.

What kind of recording works best?

One tonal recording: a sung note, a bowed note, an old synth, an organ, a pluck, a phone memo. If a sound has no clear pitch, ANDOR OSP asks.

Do I need to map zones or set loop points?

No. The root is detected and the sustain continues the recording itself. It is playable before the analysis finishes.

Can I use a folder of samples?

Yes. Pitches, velocity layers and round robins are sorted automatically, and layers are blended so boundaries don't jump.

Does it need an account or the internet?

No. It works offline, and your audio never leaves your machine.

Will a project sound the same next week?

Yes. All variation comes from stored seeds, so bounces render identically every time.
