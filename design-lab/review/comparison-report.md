# Comparison report — round 1 (broad exploration)

All images share the production layout, values, approved knob, halos and text. Open
`review/index.html` to switch directions, compare side by side, use the before/after
slider, zoom, and view actual sizes. Scores are design judgements made at 100 % and 200 %,
not measurements.

## A. Overview

**What was explored:**
- Six materials (M1–M6).
- Four button families (A1–A4), each in five states.
- Four tab families (B1–B4).
- Three fader caps on three tracks (C1–C3 × T1–T3), each at five positions plus hover and drag.
- Six macro-panel surfaces (D1–D6).
- Four enclosure treatments (E1–E4).
- Five small details (F).
- Six full-GUI directions, each combining one choice per family.

**How:**
- Every surface is real geometry: slabs, bevels, lips, grooves, recesses and lens LEDs.
- One studio lights everything (the approved knob's rig) at one calibrated exposure. Ivory base colours are calibrated so that Direction 01 reproduces today's colours.
- The plugin's own drawing is composited on top, exactly as drawn. Only its ink (text and icons) moves onto the new surfaces.

**What did not change:**
- Layout, controls, knob, values, typography, waveforms, halos.
- Nothing is integrated.

## B. The six directions

| | Direction | Families | What changes, materially |
|---|---|---|---|
| 01 | Original Refined | E1 · D1 · A4 · B1 · production slider | The same colours, now physical. The housing has a real rounded edge and shadow; cards are raised slabs with soft bevels; displays sit in real bezels; the keybed is a recess. Keys become hybrid flat. Closest to today. |
| 02 | Warm Ceramic | E1 + satin ceramic cards · D3 · A1 · B1 · C1/T1 | A satin sheen on cards and macro panel, and soft physical ivory keys that visibly press. The ivory fader cap in a hairline groove is quiet and kin to the knob. |
| 03 | Brushed Ivory | E3 · D2 · A1 · B1 · C1/T2 · recessed divider | Brushed grain on every light surface. It is invisible at 100 % and faintly felt at 200 %. The ivory cap rides in a recessed slot with a software value fill. |
| 04 | Warm Aluminium | E1 · D4 · A3 · B1 · C2/T3 · seam | A champagne brushed-metal macro plate behind the ivory knobs, metal keys with a lit foot when on, and a metal cap on a metal guide. The richest, and the most "hardware". |
| 05 | Graphite Contrast | E4 · D5 · A2 (graphite grooves) · B1 · C3/T2 · lens LEDs | The macros sit in a charcoal inset with light labels. The keybed is graphite; the keys are inset with dark grooves; the fader is graphite. The most dramatic. |
| 06 | Hybrid Premium | E1 · D6 · A2 · B1 · C2/T2 · lens LEDs · recessed divider | Today's warm housing. The macro section becomes a shallow recess with a satin floor. Keys are precision insets with a lit groove when on; tabs stay flat; one brushed-aluminium cap rides in a recessed slot. |

## C. Component findings

**Buttons.**
- A2 (precision inset) is the clearest "on" of the four. The groove lights and the face warms, while the panel stays flat and quiet. It works at 100 % even on the 27 px M/S keys.
- A1 (soft ivory) is the most tactile and kin to the knob's satin. Pressed and on read through height, not only colour. At 100 % its edge can look slightly soft.
- A3 (metal) is handsome on its own but turns the cards greyer. Its lit foot ring is a nice idea that reads as decoration at 100 %.
- A4 (hybrid flat) is the lightest. It is good for small secondary keys and too faint as a primary key.

**Tabs.**
- B1 (refined flat: a crisper 2 px underline with round ends, the selected word at full contrast) is the cleanest. It leaves the dense modulation editor alone.
- B2 (shallow inset strip) is legible and calm, but adds a second recessed shape next to the envelope display.
- B4 (precision keys) is the most "hardware" and starts to compete with the graph.
- B3's lit inlay is subtle but needs 200 % to read.
- **Recommendation: keep flat (B1).**

**Fader.**
- C2 (brushed aluminium) is the single best place for metal. A small precise object gives one premium accent, and the GUI stays warm.
- C1 (ivory) is the safest pairing with the knobs.
- C3 (graphite) needs the graphite direction around it.
- Tracks:
  - **T2** (recessed slot, software fill) gives the cap a reason to exist and reads best at 100 %;
  - **T1** (hairline groove) is closest to today;
  - **T3** (metal guide) reads as a fine rail at 200 % but is faint at 100 %, and the fill sits on top of it.

**Macro panel.**
- **D6** (shallow recess, satin floor) is the right idea, but at 0.6 mm it is too subtle. At 100 % it is hard to tell from D1, and at 200 % only a soft lip separates it. The "one physical section" effect needs a deeper recess (≈ 1.0–1.2 mm), a slightly darker floor, or both. This is the first thing to refine in round 2.
- D2 and D3 are pleasant but barely distinguishable from D1 at 100 %.
- D4 (aluminium) is beautiful at 200 % and makes the area colder.
- D5 (graphite) is striking. The knobs pop, but the knob tick dots and LED beads vanish unless redrawn light, and it competes with the dark displays.

**Background.**
- E1 (enhanced current) already lifts the whole GUI: real bevels, contact shadows, bezels, keybed recess.
- E2's powder coat is just perceptible at 200 %.
- E3's brushing disappears at 100 %.
- E4 (graphite accents) is effective in small doses; the graphite keybed is the most successful part.

**Details.**
- **Lens LEDs:** a clear improvement over the glossy beads. Their light agrees with the knob.
- **Recessed divider:** a cleaner separation than a painted line.
- **Metal seam:** too subtle to matter.
- **Button wells:** these are A2.
- **Recessed track:** this is T2.

Reasoning in brief:
- 00 loses mainly on realism and lighting consistency (the knob versus painted edges).
- 01 shows how much is gained by geometry alone.
- 02 and 03 are elegant but differ little from 01 at 100 %.
- 04 and 05 add character at the cost of warmth and restraint, and D5 / A3 / C3 each need follow-up work (light labels, tick dots, a greyer card tone).
- 06 combines the clearest active state (A2), the single metal accent (C2), the lens LEDs and the macro recess (D6, still too shallow as rendered), while keeping today's warmth. Its hierarchy score assumes the deeper recess proposed for round 2.

## E. The specific questions

- **Does brushed ivory genuinely look better than the current matte surface?** At 100 %, no: the grain disappears (as the spec asked, "barely perceptible"). At 200 % it adds a faint richness. It is worth keeping only on a small area (the macro floor), not across the window.
- **Does brushed aluminium add quality or make the GUI colder?** As a plate (D4) or on keys (A3), colder. As one fader cap (C2), it adds quality without changing the temperature.
- **Do physical tabs improve usability, or are flat tabs cleaner?** Flat tabs are cleaner. B2 and B4 do not make the selected tab clearer than B1's crisper underline, and they crowd the graph.
- **Are realistic buttons easier to distinguish when active?** Yes, if the state changes form and not only colour:
  - A2's lit groove plus the warm face, and A1's lower pressed height, beat today's pink fill;
  - A4 (flat) is no better than today.
- **Does the volume slider feel more premium?** Yes, clearly. A real cap in a recessed slot (C2/T2 or C1/T2) is the biggest single upgrade per pixel.
- **Does the macro panel still complement the dark waveform displays?** D1–D3 and D6 do. A deeper D6 would echo the display bezels best. D5 competes with them, because it is a third dark mass. D4 is neutral.
- **Are any components becoming visually too heavy?**
  - D5 (graphite inset);
  - A2 with graphite grooves (Direction 05);
  - B4 keys;
  - D4's plate.
- **Is the overall design still unmistakably ANDOR/OSP?** 01, 02, 03 and 06: yes. 04 drifts towards generic hardware. 05 drifts towards a dark-UI kit.

## F. Potential problems

| Concern | Where | Note |
|---|---|---|
| Too metallic | 04 (D4 + A3 + T3) | One metal accent is enough (C2). |
| Too busy | 05 (D5 + graphite grooves + graphite keybed) | Three dark accents compete with three dark displays. |
| Reduced contrast | D5 | Tick dots and LED beads lost; labels need light variants. A3 keys: lower icon contrast on grey metal. |
| Conflicts with the approved knob | D4, D5 | The knob was lit and coloured for ivory surroundings. On metal it reads stuck-on; on graphite it pops (pleasing, but a different product). |
| Unnecessary complexity | E2/E3 tiles across the window, B2–B4 | Grain that disappears at 100 % costs assets for no visible gain. |
| Groove weight | A2 | The first render's groove read as an outline. It was refined (1.0 px, warmer); judge at 100 %. |

## G. Technical feasibility

See `technical-feasibility.md`. In short:
- Buttons, tabs, the fader cap and LEDs become small 9-slice frames or sprites (well under 1 MB in total).
- The macro panel and enclosure become 9-slice edges plus vector gradients, and a small tile only where grain must be visible.
- Tabs B1 and dividers stay vector.
- No runtime 3D. No figures are claimed beyond arithmetic.
